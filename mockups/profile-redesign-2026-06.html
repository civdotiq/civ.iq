Design notes — what changed and why
  1. Content replaces navigation. The old page was eight "Explore" cards — a menu styled as content. Every section now shows its top 3–5 records inline (votes, bills, donors, news) with an "All →" link. A citizen learns something without clicking.
  2. Every number gets a baseline. "20 votes cast" became "20 of 21 · 95% participation · House avg 96%". "$9.7M raised" gets "House median $1.8M". Per intelligence-layer rules: numbers without peer comparison are noise.
  3. Sticky section nav for wayfinding. Replaces the card grid as the navigation layer. Active state is interactive blue (#3ea2d4), 3px border per emphasis hierarchy. Always visible on a long page.
  4. Contact promoted to primary action. Contacting your representative is the #1 citizen task; it was a small phone number in the old hero. Now a black primary button, persistent in the identity block.
  5. Sidebar carries reference tasks. Committees (all 4 visible, linked per link-density rule), next election, district snapshot with address-lookup caveat (amber, per address-not-ZIP rule), delegation, data sources.
  6. Identity block tightened. Name in sentence case (all-caps destroys word shape at 40px+), term/seniority/committee count as scannable facts, party chip in Democrat blue #2563eb used only for party.
  7. Intelligence shown with its required metadata. Confidence meter, sample size, dataAsOf, methodology link, no-causation disclaimer — inline, not hidden behind a click.
  8. Provenance per section. Each block footer names its government source, replacing the single generic "Data Sources" band.
  9. Border hierarchy used for scanning. 2px structural containers, 1px row dividers, 3px only on active/emphasis (section nav, insight rail). The old page used 2px everywhere, so nothing stood out.
